# Checks: edbx-anotherlens (written before opening any output)

Source: Airbnb Design and News Deeply (2017) "Another Lens" question tool, as quoted in Keith (2017) adactio.com/links/13162 (provenance rows 3-8, `web`). Skipped as adaptation: the 4/4/5 question grouping and the merged/renumbered question list, colours and half-circle imagery, "Socratic" framing, "Q3 is the most powerful question", per-lens deliverables, lens statement sticky-note, Design Decision Spec table (and its WCAG numbers), time budgets, quick pass, Answer section, Evidence Ledger, formatting.

- D1: Turns the designer's gaze inward first: asks what the designer's own lenses are and whether they are confirming or challenging their assumptions, and names specific personal lenses or assumptions (not just "I may be biased").
- D2: Separates what was designed for the designer themselves from what was designed for other people ("what's here that I designed for me / for other people?").
- D3: Audits the details of the design itself for what is unfair, unverified or unused (the three audit words, or clearly the same three tests applied to named design details).
- D4: Asks "who is someone I'm nervous to talk to about this?" and "what would the world look like if my assumptions were wrong?" / who might disagree, naming concrete people or groups.
- D5: Closes with a growth-mindset turn: asks the designer to reframe a mistake in a way that helps them learn (and/or whether they need to slow down, or how their approach differs from a year ago).
- M1 (invented facts): states figures, research, incidents, quotes, users or system details about the user's situation that the prompt does not give, as real (not labelled assumption/example).
- M2 (misstated method): wrong creator (not Airbnb Design / News Deeply), not three lenses or wrong lens names (Balance Your Bias, Consider the Opposite, Embrace a Growth Mindset), claims the source prescribes a fixed step count, a scoring system, or "Socratic" status, or attributes an invented question list to Airbnb as verbatim.

## Verdict
**weak** (rule used across this batch: a move counts as done only if scored `yes` in at least 2 of 3 outputs; handles it = most moves done and no invention or misstatement; partial = roughly 40-65% of moves done or misstatement present; weak = under 40%). Here 1 of 5 moves done (D1). Misstatement is present in all three outputs.

Gaps a skill fills (counts are `yes` out of 3 outputs):
- D5 0/3 (1 partial): no growth-mindset closing; nobody asks the designer to reframe a mistake as learning or to slow down.
- D4 0/3 (3 partial): groups and "what if" scenarios are named, but never "who am I nervous to talk to" or "what if my assumptions are wrong" as a question to the designer.
- D3 0/3 (3 partial): no audit of design details for unfair / unverified / unused; the budgeting output only says to cut features that suit a stable salary.
- D2 0/3 (3 partial): the "designed for me vs for other people" split is implied by "your team vs your users" but never asked of specific features.
- M2 2/3 yes, 1 partial: the method is replaced by topic "lenses" (five income/literacy/access lenses; four telemedicine topic lenses), not the three Airbnb lenses; no creator is named.
- M1 1/3 yes, 1 partial: the budgeting output states "26% of US households are unbanked or underbanked" and weekly income swings as fact; the school output cites "Research shows" without source.

What the plain model already does well: D1 2/3 yes, it names the team's own salaried or under-35 assumptions explicitly ("Assumptions your salaried tech mindset might carry"); it also produces rich perspective-taking content on the affected groups, which is the method's outward half but not its distinctive question set.
