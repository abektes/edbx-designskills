# Checks: edbx-cider (written before opening any output)

Source: Oleson, Solomon, Perdriau & Ko (2023), Teaching inclusive design skills with the CIDER assumption elicitation technique, TOCHI 30(1) (`oleson2023teaching`, provenance rows 1-9). Skipped as adaptation: the "This design assumes the user [X]" sentence frame, the minimum of 5 assumptions over 3 of 4 categories, the named-person (Mariam-style) requirement, the 3-5 redesign count and four approach types, clustering/tagging/surprise-flagging in Stage E, solo-mode Stage R from Stage C, the Inclusive Design Commitment, mindset check, Answer section, Evidence Ledger, output formatting.

- D1: Names the five stages as Critique, Imagine, Design, Expand, Repeat and runs them in that order (row 2).
- D2: Critique stage lists assumptions the design makes about users' abilities, capacity, environment and resources (rows 4, 9).
- D3: Imagine stage takes ONE chosen assumption and writes a scenario of a user for whom that assumption is wrong and the design breaks down (row 5).
- D4: Design stage brainstorms changes to the artifact that remove reliance on that assumption (row 6).
- D5: Expand stage pools several participants' assumption lists into one shared list with duplicates removed (row 7).
- D6: Repeat stage picks a different assumption (one not already used/surfaced) and runs Imagine and Design again (row 8).
- M1 (invented facts): states figures, research, incidents, quotes, users or system details about the user's situation that the prompt does not give, as real (not labelled assumption/example).
- M2 (misstated method): wrong creators (e.g. "Horowitz" as author, or a different creator), wrong acronym expansion or stage names/count, claims CIDER is validated in professional practice, calls GenderMag its foundation, attributes the 5-assumption/3-category quota, named person, "3-5 redesigns" or four approach types to the published method, or says the paper has nine/other categories wrongly.

## Verdict
**weak** (rule: a move counts as done only if scored `yes` in at least 2 of 3 outputs; handles it = most moves done and no invention or misstatement; partial = roughly 40-65% done or misstatement present; weak = under 40%). Here 1 of 6 moves done (D4 only). M1 present in 2 of 3 (partial in the third); M2 present in 2 of 3.

Gaps a skill fills (counts are `yes` out of 3 outputs):
- D1 0/3: the five stages (Critique, Imagine, Design, Expand, Repeat) never appear; two outputs invent a different expansion (telehealth: "Context, Inclusion & Impact, Design audit, Ethical evaluation, Recommendations"; voting: "Context, Impact, Design, Ethics, Recommendations") and the third names no stages.
- D3 0/3: no output picks one assumption and writes a scenario of a user for whom it is wrong; they give group impact tables, at most a one-line intersectional example.
- D5 0/3 and D6 0/3: no pooled list of assumptions and no second Imagine/Design cycle on a different assumption.
- D2 1/3: only telehealth lists assumptions the design makes about users; the others list barriers per step or group.

What the plain model already does well:
- D4 3/3: redesign ideas that remove reliance on an exclusionary requirement (broader ID, guest checkout, optional street address) are plentiful.
- Wide coverage of excluded groups, and ability/environment/resources barriers, even without the assumption framing.
