(2021) p. 14 for the three named theories and p. 28 for the Gispen attribution (rows 3, 17). The template file itself was not read in provenance, so its per-lens questions are not checkable. Skipped as adaptation: the Intention / Effect / Design labels paired with the three theories, per-lens questions, Step 0 Stakeholder and Power Map, Non-Obvious Harms Inventory, Creator Impact, Universal Law Test, Triad Conflict Matrix, Cross-Lens Synthesis, Normative Design Statement, Priority Recommendation, "2 ideas per lens", Assess/Generate as separate modes, "greatest good for the greatest number", hashtags/See Also, Answer section, Evidence Ledger, output formatting.

- D1: Starts by writing the design goal in one fixed, short structure (the template's format) before any ethical assessment (rows 5, 14).
- D2: Assesses that design goal with the three main normative ethics views: virtue ethics, consequentialism (consequentialist) and deontology, side by side (rows 3, 5).
- D3: Then uses the same theories as ideation questions to generate design ideas that morally improve the design (row 5).
- D4: States that the questions have no absolute answer and the views are not ranked or collapsed into one verdict (row 6).
- D5: Treats the method as a short team worksheet (about 30-60 minutes, template, post-its) with four steps (browse, goal, assess, generate), not a long written report (row 14).
- M1 (invented facts): states figures, research, incidents, quotes, users or system details about the user's situation that the prompt does not give, as real (not labelled assumption/example).
- M2 (misstated method): credits a creator other than Jet Gispen / Ethics for Designers; names a different or larger set of theories (e.g. adds care ethics, justice, rights) as the method's own; presents the Intention/Effect/Design mapping, a Triad Conflict Matrix, Universal Law Test, Creator Impact, stakeholder-power map, Normative Design Statement or "2 ideas per lens" as part of the published method; gives a step count other than the four-step worksheet; claims the method is validated or produces a verdict.

## Verdict
**weak** (rule: a move counts as done only if scored `yes` in at least 2 of 3 outputs; handles it = most moves done and no invention or misstatement; partial = roughly 40-65% done or misstatement present; weak = under 40%). Here 1 of 5 moves done (D2 only). M1 present in 2 of 3 (partial in the third); M2 present in 1 of 3 (yes), partial in a second.

Note on D2: the dating and policing prompts themselves name the three lenses, so those two `yes` scores credit the model for following the prompt; given only the method name (attention-economy prompt) the model named none of the three theories.

Gaps a skill fills (counts are `yes` out of 3 outputs):
- D1 0/3: no output writes a design goal in a fixed short format first; they open with context, stakeholder maps or value elicitation.
- D2 2/3 (prompt-supplied): the attention-economy output redefines the method as stakeholders, values, norms and design requirements, with no ethical theories at all.
- D3 0/3: no output uses the theories as ideation questions to improve the design; recommendations are generic ethical-redesign lists (two partial).
- D4 0/3: no statement that the questions have no absolute answer; outputs deliver verdicts per lens and a consensus (two partial for noting tension).
- D5 0/3: all are long reports; none treats the method as a 30-60 minute template and post-it exercise.
