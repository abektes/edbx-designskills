# Humane Design Alternatives — Positive Design Patterns

For each human sensitivity, concrete design alternatives that support rather than exploit. Use these when generating redesign proposals in Step 3.

---

## Attention — Enabled to Find Focus and Peace

**Replace infinite scroll with batched loading.** Show 10–20 items, then present a "Load more" or "Continue?" prompt. This restores the user's ability to choose to stop.

**Introduce natural stopping points.** Build in chapter breaks, session summaries, or "you're caught up" moments. The absence of stopping points is an exploitation pattern — their presence is a humane pattern.

**Batch notifications.** Instead of real-time notification delivery, offer digest mode — a summary of notifications delivered at user-chosen intervals. Let users define their own interruption budget.

**Offer focus mode.** Provide a setting that suppresses all non-essential visual and auditory stimuli. Gray out the interface, remove animations, simplify navigation.

**Remove autoplay.** Require user initiation for every piece of content. The pause between videos is a moment of agency.

**Transparent time awareness.** Show users how long they've been engaging. Not as a shaming mechanism, but as awareness support — "You've been scrolling for 20 minutes."

---

## Emotional — Enabled to Process and Adapt

**Remove public like/reaction counts.** Keep the ability to react, but make counts visible only to the creator, not the audience. This decouples self-worth from quantified approval.

**Replace shame with encouragement.** Instead of "You've broken your 30-day streak," try "Great to see you back! Every session counts." Frame absence as normal, not failure.

**Offer emotional check-ins.** Before showing content, ask "How are you feeling?" Use the response to adjust content selection — not to exploit mood, but to avoid worsening it.

**Remove aspirational defaults.** If filters or editing tools are offered, default to natural/minimal. Make enhanced versions opt-in, not opt-out.

**Design for emotional resilience.** Provide resources, not just content. If a user is engaging with difficult content, offer context, support resources, or the option to step away.

---

## Sensemaking — Enabled to Find Truth and Clarity

**Label content types clearly.** Distinguish news, opinion, advertisement, user-generated content, and algorithmic recommendation with clear, consistent labels. Use words, not just icons.

**Show provenance.** Let users trace content to its source. Who created it? When? Has it been modified? Was it algorithmically recommended or organically surfaced?

**Inject diverse perspectives.** In recommendation systems, deliberately surface content that challenges the user's existing views. Not as the dominant experience, but as a visible minority.

**Provide context alongside content.** Show related articles, fact-checks, or background information. Don't let content stand alone when it could be misleading.

**Make algorithmic reasoning visible.** Tell users why they're seeing what they're seeing. "This was recommended because you read X" is transparency. "Recommended for you" is opacity.

**Reduce information velocity.** Offer a "slow feed" option that surfaces fewer, higher-quality items rather than a high-volume stream optimized for engagement.

---

## Decision-making — Enabled to Act with Agency and Control

**Equal-weight choices.** When presenting options (accept/decline, subscribe/cancel), make buttons the same size, color, and prominence. No visual hierarchy manipulation.

**Transparent consequences.** Before any action with consequences, show what will happen in plain language. "Clicking 'Continue' will charge your card $14.99/month starting immediately."

**Symmetrical friction.** Make the effort required to start something equal to the effort required to stop it. If subscription is 1-click, cancellation should be too.

**Clear defaults.** Default to the option that serves the user's interest, not the organization's. Make changing defaults easy and visible.

**Reflection prompts.** Before high-stakes decisions (purchase, data sharing, subscription), introduce a brief pause. "Are you sure?" is not a dark pattern when it's genuine — it's a dark pattern when it's performative.

**Undo capability.** Provide a grace period for reversible actions. The ability to undo is the opposite of forced commitment.

---

## Social Reasoning — Enabled to Connect Meaningfully

**Private by default.** Make social activity visible only when the user explicitly chooses to share. Default to privacy; make publicity opt-in.

**Remove social scoreboards.** If follower counts, friend counts, or social scores are displayed, make them hideable. The number is less important than the quality of connection.

**Design for depth, not breadth.** Encourage meaningful interaction over wide networking. Features that deepen existing relationships are more humane than features that expand reach.

**Support consent in social features.** Before tagging, mentioning, or adding someone, require their consent. Social features that bypass consent create social pressure.

**Offer non-comparative social experiences.** Provide modes where users can interact without seeing follower counts, likes, or status indicators. Just people, talking.

---

## Group Dynamics — Enabled to Build Strong Communities

**Amplify bridge content.** In recommendation systems, identify and promote content that builds understanding across groups rather than content that deepens division.

**Design for accountability.** In group interactions, ensure that identity and reputation follow behavior. Anonymity without accountability enables dehumanization.

**Support community norms.** Give communities tools to set and enforce their own standards, not just platform-wide rules. Healthy communities self-regulate when given the tools.

**Visible diversity.** Show users that multiple perspectives exist within their community, not just the dominant one. Countervailing voices should not be algorithmically invisible.

**Reduce outrage incentives.** If the platform rewards content that generates strong negative reactions (ratio, quote-tweet pile-ons), redesign the reward structure. Engagement is not the same as value.

**Support exit as well as voice.** Give users clean, simple ways to leave groups, mute conversations, or reduce exposure without social cost. The ability to leave is as important as the ability to participate.
