# Related Methods — EDB Toolkit Connections

Worrystorming is part of the Ethical Design Box (EDB) skillset. It connects to other skills in specific, complementary ways.

---

## Companion Skill Relationships

### edb-motivation-matrix
The Motivation Matrix maps five motivational types (Achievement, Social Acceptance, Fear, Power, Incentive) across user contexts. Worrystorming surfaces the ethical concerns that arise when those motivations are exploited.
**Sequence:** Run the Motivation Matrix to understand motivational mechanics, then Worrystorming to surface what could go wrong when those mechanics are pushed too far.

### edb-responsible-design-prism
The Prism diagnoses where a design currently sits on the dark-to-responsible spectrum. Worrystorming helps prevent ending up on the dark side by identifying concerns before they become patterns.
**Sequence:** Run Worrystorming early in the project to surface concerns, then the Prism later to verify the design avoids them.

### edb-anotherlens
Another Lens surfaces the designer's internal biases and worldview assumptions. Worrystorming surfaces the external consequences those biases might cause at scale.
**Sequence:** Run Another Lens first to name the lenses the team carries, then Worrystorm with those self-awareness insights active.

### edb-humane-design-guide
The Humane Design Guide provides a structured sensitivity taxonomy (Attention, Emotional, Sensemaking, Decision-making, Social Reasoning, Group Dynamics). Worrystorming generates the raw concerns that can then be organized using that taxonomy.
**Sequence:** Run Worrystorming to generate broad concerns, then the Humane Design Guide to map them onto specific sensitivities and generate alternatives.

### edb-cider
CIDER audits who is excluded by design through assumption elicitation. Worrystorming generates the worries about exclusion — both who is excluded and what happens when they are.
**Sequence:** Run Worrystorming to surface exclusion-related concerns, then CIDER to systematically address the assumptions causing them.

## Future Method Connections

These methods are referenced in the source material and may be added to the EDB skillset:

- **Empathic Walkthrough** — Walk through the product as a vulnerable user after Worrystorming to test whether worries were real
- **Moral Agent** — Assign moral responsibility to the worries surfaced in the session

## Recommended Sequences

**Full ethical audit:**
1. edb-anotherlens (self-awareness)
2. edb-cider (inclusion check)
3. edb-worrystorming (concern surfacing)
4. edb-humane-design-guide (sensitivity mapping)
5. edb-responsible-design-prism (responsibility placement)
6. edb-motivation-matrix (motivational mechanics)

**Early-stage ethical exploration:**
1. edb-anotherlens
2. edb-worrystorming
3. edb-cider

**Pre-launch ethical review:**
1. edb-worrystorming
2. edb-humane-design-guide
3. edb-responsible-design-prism

**Quick concern scan:**
1. edb-worrystorming (Steps 2–3 only: generate and cluster worries)

## Worrystorming as Capstone

Worrystorming is Method #100 — the final method in *Universal Methods of Ethical Design*. It can function as a capstone practice that synthesizes insights from the other methods. After running individual methods, a Worrystorming session can integrate their findings into a unified concern map with prioritized ethical objectives.

The method is explicitly consequentialist — it asks "what happens downstream, at scale, over time?" This makes it a natural complement to the deontological (principle-based) framing of methods like Another Lens and the virtue-based framing of the Humane Design Guide.
