*A practical template for ethical assessment at a glance.*

**Instructions:** Assess your activity or creation by addressing four key dimensions. For each spectrum, mark your assessment (use −, −−, neutral, +, or ++ to indicate position), consider the observable outcomes, and note what else you need to know. After completing all four dimensions, write a synthesis that considers the full picture — no single spectrum determines ethical soundness.

| There is limited benefit to individuals. What already exists benefits people more. | ←─────────────────────→ | [ mark here ] | There is great benefit to individuals. There is more benefit than what already exists. |
| The benefit is limited to few select people. | ←─────────────────────→ | [ mark here ] | Large numbers and multiple (or all) groups benefit. |

- How do we measure the effects?
- How might we move toward the positive end?

## DIMENSION 2 — HOW IS IT HARMFUL?

| Spectrum | − | Assessment | + |
|----------|---|------------|---|
| There is great harm to individuals. There is more harm than what already exists. | ←─────────────────────→ | [ mark here ] | There is limited harm to individuals. Harm caused is less than what already exists (alternatives). |
| Large numbers and multiple (or all) groups are harmed. | ←─────────────────────→ | [ mark here ] | The harm is limited to few people. |
| Harm is certain to occur or impossible to prevent. | ←─────────────────────→ | [ mark here ] | Harm is unlikely to occur. Potential harm is likely to be successfully prevented. |

**Reflection:**
- What would we expect to observe if harm occurs?
- What indicators could help track this?
- How might harm be mitigated, remedied, or prevented?
- Is the harm enduring or reversible?

## DIMENSION 3 — HOW FAIR IS IT?

*Note: The first spectrum is neutral — neither equal nor unequal distribution is inherently better. Fairness depends on the broader context.*

| Spectrum | ← | Assessment | → |
|----------|---|------------|---|
| Certain people or groups are affected more than others. | ←─────────────────────→ | [ mark here ] | All people and groups are affected equally. |
| Those who are harmed are also less advantaged in society. Those who benefit have privilege in society. | ←─────────────────────→ | [ mark here ] | There is no more harm to those who are less advantaged in society. Those who benefit have greater need. |
| The burden faced by those who are (most) harmed is not acceptable or justifiable. | ←─────────────────────→ | [ mark here ] | All things considered, the burden faced by those who are (most) harmed is acceptable or justifiable. |

**Reflection:**
- Are impacts equitable in distribution?
- Are vulnerable groups disproportionately affected?
- What makes this distribution acceptable or unacceptable?
- Reference: Value Cards — Justice, Dignity

## DIMENSION 4 — HOW EMPOWERING IS IT?

| Spectrum | − | Assessment | + |
|----------|---|------------|---|
| People's abilities to make informed choices for themselves are reduced. | ←─────────────────────→ | [ mark here ] | People's abilities to make informed choices for themselves are unimpaired or increased. |
| People's control of aspect(s) of their lives is removed. | ←─────────────────────→ | [ mark here ] | People's control of aspect(s) of their lives is retained or enhanced. |
| There are activities that our actions or creations remove freedom to do. | ←─────────────────────→ | [ mark here ] | Our creation does not coerce, manipulate, or pressure people, and limits others' ability to do so. |

**Reflection:**
- Does this work undermine or enhance autonomy?
- Is freedom reduced through coercion, manipulation, exploitation, or psychological pressure?
- Could autonomy limits be justified by preventing greater harm?
- Reference: Value Cards — Autonomy, Responsibility
