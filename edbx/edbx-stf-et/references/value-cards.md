# Value Explainer Cards

Card names follow the Stanford Ethics Toolkit (Travaglianti & Both, 2025); descriptions are this package's own paraphrase.

Value Cards turn an abstract value into something you can observe. Each card gives a short working definition and three spectra; each spectrum describes what it looks like in practice when the value is eroded and when it is strengthened.

Use these cards throughout the stf-et chain:
- **Impacts Explorer** — to name the values impacted by ripple effects
- **Ethics Frame** — to define values driving your work and assess how they are impacted
- **Ethics Gauge** — the Fairness and Empowerment dimensions draw directly on these cards
- **Weighing Options** — to articulate what each option prioritizes

## WELL-BEING
*How well individuals and society are doing: health, happiness and the chance to grow.*

Looking at what you do and make … **How might it affect well-being?**

| DEGRADING | PROMOTING |
|-----------|-----------|
| The creation damages bodies or minds, or encourages habits and circumstances that leave people worse off. | The creation supports bodily and mental health and the habits and circumstances that keep people well. |
| The creation breeds frustration, stress or unease, and people end up less happy. | The creation brings enjoyment and a sense of satisfaction, and people end up happier. |
| The creation holds people back, so they stay where they are and cannot progress. | The creation helps people learn, develop and gain capability, so they can become more of what they could be. |

## JUSTICE
*Fairness in who receives the benefits and who carries the burdens of what a society offers.*

Looking at what you do and make … **How might it contribute to justice?**

| DEGRADING | PROMOTING |
|-----------|-----------|
| Some people or groups bear more of the downsides of your actions or creation than others. | No person or group ends up carrying a disproportionate share of the effects. |
| If the creation succeeds, it entrenches existing bias or pushes certain people or groups further to the margins. | If the creation succeeds, it lessens existing bias or exclusion. |
| The people who are hurt are already the most marginalized, while the people who gain are already powerful and privileged. | The people who are currently marginalized or most in need gain the most. |

---

## TRUST
*Willingly relying on someone else's ability and good intentions where our own interests are at stake.*

Looking at what you do and make … **How might it influence trust?**

| DEGRADING | PROMOTING |
|-----------|-----------|
| The needs, interests and expectations of the people affected are ignored. | You actively seek out and account for the needs, interests and expectations of the people affected. |
| What people expect does not line up with what the creation can and cannot do, or with the values behind it. | You set honest expectations about what you do and make, including its limits, and own that responsibility. |
| Affected people have little means to voice concerns or hold you to account. | Affected people have real means to be heard and to hold you to account. |

---

## PRIVACY
*Control over how personal information moves, including protection from unwanted intrusion.*

Looking at what you do and make … **How might it impact privacy?**

| DEGRADING | PROMOTING |
|-----------|-----------|
| Personal or sensitive information reaches parties who should not have it, or becomes public. | Personal or sensitive data is not collected at all, or is protected well enough that it is not exposed or misused. |
| After people hand over data, they cannot withdraw access or steer what happens to it. | People can withdraw consent or limit use at any time without penalty, and keep a say over how their data is used. |
| As the creation spreads beyond its original purpose or reaches many people, privacy expectations in society shrink. | As the creation spreads beyond its original purpose or reaches many people, privacy expectations in society grow. |

---

## DIGNITY
*The worth every person has simply by being a person, which calls for recognition and respect.*

Looking at what you do and make … **How might it preserve dignity?**

| DEGRADING | PROMOTING |
|-----------|-----------|
| The creation pushes, pressures or deceives people, or lets others do so. | The creation does not push, pressure or deceive, and makes it harder for others to do so. |
| The creation suits people of certain abilities and backgrounds and leaves everyone else out. | Differences in needs, abilities and culture are thoughtfully and sufficiently accommodated. |
| People are used as tools for your goals and exploited, by the creation or by the conditions it produces. | The work is built around what people need and ends up giving them more power over their own lives. |

---

## VIRTUES
*Settled character traits that lead a person to act well.*

Looking at what you do and make … **How might it relate to virtues?**

| DEGRADING | PROMOTING |
|-----------|-----------|
| The creation wears down good conduct such as honesty, empathy or generosity, and pushes people toward worse character. | The creation encourages good conduct and helps people improve themselves and mature. |
| The creation takes away chances to practise a virtue, or makes practising it more expensive. | The creation opens up chances to practise a virtue, or makes doing so easier. |
| The creation steers people away from thinking about what their own actions mean and from owning them. | The creation prompts people to think about what their own actions mean and to own them. |

---

## AUTONOMY
*The capacity to run one's own life according to one's own judgment.*

Looking at what you do and make … **How might it impact autonomy?**

| DEGRADING | PROMOTING |
|-----------|-----------|
| The creation or action settles matters that people should decide for themselves. | The creation or action leaves people real choices, with real consequences, in their own hands. |
| The creation invites misuse, excessive use or reliance, and leaving it becomes hard or costly. | The creation supports responsible use and lets people stay self-reliant. |
| At scale, the creation would leave people little practical way to decline or step away. | At scale, the creation would strengthen people's self-direction and keep opting out easy. |

---

## RESPONSIBILITY
*Being aware of, caring about and answerable for what one's choices and actions cause.*

Looking at what you do and make … **How might it exercise responsibility?**

| DEGRADING | PROMOTING |
|-----------|-----------|
| There is little way to detect misuse of the creation or the harm it may cause. | There are adequate ways to detect misuse of the creation and the harm it may cause. |
| There is little way to respond once misuse or harm is found. | There are workable ways to respond once misuse or harm is found. |
| Nobody has committed to reducing the harm the creation or your actions cause. | There is a firm commitment to reducing that harm, including when use is broad and widespread, and to meeting what people can reasonably expect of you. |

---

## RELATIONSHIPS
*The bonds between people, which offer benefits that exist only within those bonds.*

Looking at what you do and make … **How might it impact interpersonal relationships?**

| DEGRADING | PROMOTING |
|-----------|-----------|
| Using the creation, people understand one another less, communicate badly and grow apart. | Using the creation, people understand one another better, communicate well and stay connected. |
| Harmful attitudes and behaviors such as conflict, polarization or isolation start or get worse. | Constructive attitudes and behaviors such as give-and-take, empathy and community start or get stronger. |
| The creation changes how people deal with each other in ways that wear away what their relationships give them. | The creation changes how people deal with each other in ways that protect or deepen what their relationships give them. |

## Creating Your Own Value Card

Use this structure to define additional values specific to your work or context. Custom cards can cover moral values not listed above, non-moral organizational values (efficiency, user-centeredness, environmental sustainability), or domain-specific principles (legal standards, humanitarian principles, field-specific codes of ethics).

Each custom card should include:
1. **Name** — the value, in a word or phrase
2. **Definition** — a clear sentence defining what the value means (need not be philosophical; put words to the idea concretely)
3. **Three spectra** — each spectrum identifies a key dimension of the value and shows what degradation and promotion look like in observable scenarios

**Template:**

```
## [VALUE NAME]
*[Definition sentence.]*

Considering your action/creations … How might it affect [value name]?

| DEGRADING | PROMOTING |
|-----------|-----------|
| Observable scenario showing value degraded | Observable scenario showing value promoted |
| Observable scenario showing value degraded | Observable scenario showing value promoted |
| Observable scenario showing value degraded | Observable scenario showing value promoted |
```
