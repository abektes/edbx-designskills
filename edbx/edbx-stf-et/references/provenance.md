# Provenance — edbx-stf-et

Stage A draft (2026-10-13). One row per element the skill attributes to a
source. Locator and anchor are what could be confirmed from web pages read in
this pass (no files downloaded). Where a page could not confirm an element,
Locator says `to verify` and the anchor is a guess marked `(guess)`. Stage B
checks each anchor on the page.

**Headline: the toolkit's detail (tool sections, spectra, value-card text,
worksheets) lives in a PDF / Mural reached through an access form. Only the
landing page could be read, so most element-level rows are `to verify`. The
landing page states no licence, so the "CC BY 4.0" claim in SKILL.md,
`references/value-cards.md` and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` is unconfirmed (see
Licence).**

## Licence

- **`travaglianti2025ethics`: unknown.** SKILL.md (line 11), `references/value-cards.md` and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` state "CC BY 4.0 — ethicstoolkit.stanford.edu". On the landing page I searched for licence, Creative Commons and CC BY and found nothing; the only rights text is "© Stanford University" and a copyright-infringement link. The site that name points to redirects to the same page. The access-form route (PDF / Mural on request) suggests the terms, if any, are inside the PDF, which could not be read. What it would mean:
  - **If the PDF is CC BY 4.0:** paraphrasing, verbatim reuse of card text, worksheets and diagrams, and shipping modified versions in a paid bundle are all allowed with attribution, a licence link and a note of changes. No block.
  - **If the PDF has no open licence (default "© Stanford"):** paraphrasing the method is fine. Reproducing the nine value cards verbatim (references/value-cards.md), the four worksheet templates, or the tool wording closely is not permitted without permission. That would block commercial redistribution of those files in a paid bundle.
  - Until checked, do not repeat "CC BY 4.0" as fact; mark it unverified. The card text and templates are the highest-risk files. Check the PDF's colophon or contact the Center (the page offers a contact form for adaptation guidance, https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLScTtQAIW68qK5qOBAiyeghxRLo0-nopxDLFpz42-RTuyrgeFA/viewform).
- **`adams1991storyspine`.** No licence stated. The six-prompt spine in SKILL.md is the toolkit's adaptation; paraphrase only, credit Adams.
- **`glenn2009futures`.** Unknown. A method idea; paraphrase and credit Glenn.
- Nothing found forbids commercial use or modification. The open question is whether any permission exists at all for verbatim card text and worksheets.

## Discrepancies

### Contradicted by the source (I read the source and it says otherwise)

1. **Phase grouping and label (weak).** SKILL.md and three-phases.md put Ethics Frame under Evaluate (2 + 2 + 1) and call the third phase "Decide". The landing page lists Ethics Frame with Future Story and Impacts Explorer (3 + 1 + 1) and labels the phases "Explore, Evaluate, Make Decisions". The labels come from image alt text and the grouping from list order, so confirm against the flowchart or the PDF before editing.

### Not found in a readable source (could not confirm; the full source is a PDF behind a form)

2. **Licence "CC BY 4.0".** Not on the landing page, which shows "© Stanford University". Stated as fact in SKILL.md, value-cards.md and THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md.
3. **"V1.1" and "Put Values Into Action".** No version or tagline on the landing page; 2025 inferred from file names.
4. **Seven of the nine value names** (Trust, Privacy, Dignity, Virtues, Autonomy, Responsibility, Relationships) and all card definitions and spectra. Only Well-Being and Justice appear, as one image-card.
5. **Future Story's six-element spine and its prompts, and the credit to Adams.** The page names neither; Adams' own spine has different prompts.
6. **Futures Wheel basis, In-to-Out / Out-to-In directions, group-specific lens.** Not on the page.
7. **Ethics Frame's nine sections, the four extent dimensions, the Gauge's four dimensions and twelve spectra, Weighing Options' sub-sections.** Only the one-line descriptions are on the page.
8. **Chain framing, time budgets, "Core premise" wording, feed-forward blocks, quality-bar counts.** Not on the page; the feed-forward blocks are presented in SKILL.md as the method's own ("this is the handoff that makes the chain work") but are probably an edbx layer.
9. **Creators and funder omitted.** Not a contradiction, but SKILL.md credits only the Center; the page names Manuela Travaglianti (project lead) and Thomas Both (designer) and funding from the Project Liberty Institute.
