# Three Phases — Explore, Evaluate, Decide

The Stanford Ethics Toolkit is organized into three phases. Each phase has a distinct purpose and set of tools. Choose tools based on what phase of your project you are in and what kind of ethical support you currently need.

---

## Phase 1: EXPLORE
*"We need to get some of the ethical considerations on the table and start the conversation."*

**Goal:** Become aware of and reflect on the ethical considerations at play.

**When to use:** Early-stage projects, new research initiatives, ideation phases, or any time the team hasn't yet structured its ethical thinking.

**Tools:**

| Tool | What it does |
|------|-------------|
| **Future Story** | Write the story of your work, looking back from the future, to imagine implications. Narrative format surfaces concerns without requiring prior analysis. |
| **Impacts Explorer** | Consider effects on people and society, and how related ethical values are promoted or degraded. Maps the full ripple effect from action to secondary consequences to values. |

**Explore phase outputs feed into:** The Evaluate phase tools, which take the surfaced concerns and assess their ethical implications systematically.

---

## Phase 2: EVALUATE
*"We'd like to get a solid assessment of the implications of our actions, and consider options in response."*

**Goal:** Assess the ethical impact of decisions and identify areas to adjust.

**When to use:** Mid-development, when the action or creation is defined and the team needs to assess its ethical implications before moving forward. Also useful as a recurring check-in as work evolves.

**Tools:**

| Tool | What it does |
|------|-------------|
| **Ethics Frame** | A single worksheet to outline ethical considerations and actions in response. Covers values, benefits, harms, who is affected, and how to expand benefits and reduce harms. Produces a Bottom Line. |
| **Ethics Gauge** | Assess the implication of your actions on a more granular level across four dimensions: beneficial, harmful, fair, empowering. Uses observable spectra for systematic assessment. |

**Note on using Value Cards here:** The Value Explainer Cards in the Appendix are most actively used during the Evaluate phase to make values concrete and observable. Reference them throughout the Ethics Frame's values sections and the Ethics Gauge's fairness and empowerment dimensions.

**Evaluate phase outputs feed into:** The Decide phase, which takes the ethical picture assembled by the Evaluate tools and uses it to compare options and make principled choices.

---

## Phase 3: DECIDE
*"We want to compare options and make principled decisions about how to move forward."*

**Goal:** Deliberate on how to address ethical dilemmas and adapt plans to drive toward key values.

**When to use:** Decision points — when two or more courses of action are under consideration and the team needs a transparent, value-aligned comparison to choose a direction.

**Tools:**

| Tool | What it does |
|------|-------------|
| **Weighing Options** | A tool to compare different options, considering the effects on society and your organization. Produces a reasoned Future Direction statement. |

---

## Choosing Where to Enter

The three phases are designed to be traversed in sequence, but entry at any phase is valid if the prior phases have been addressed by other means (prior research, existing user data, stakeholder input, etc.).

```
Situation                               → Enter at
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
No prior ethical analysis                → Phase 1 (Future Story)
Some impact mapping done                 → Phase 1 or 2
Clear impact picture, need assessment   → Phase 2 (Ethics Frame)
Need a quick ethical check              → Phase 2 (Ethics Gauge)
Decision between options needed         → Phase 3 (Weighing Options)
Full ethical assessment from scratch    → Phase 1 → 2 → 3 in sequence
```

## What These Phases Will and Won't Do

**The tools DO:**
- Help you confront key ethical issues so important concerns aren't overlooked
- Challenge you to think beyond your intentions and intuition
- Help you understand the risks so you can own them
- Help you plan for mitigation so you can fulfill your responsibilities
- Make you purposefully aim for individual and societal well-being
- Facilitate group exploration and discussion
- Help promote accountability

**The tools DON'T:**
- Tell you the right thing to do — they offer structure, but you must define the values and assess trade-offs
- Provide a complete checklist of harms — they give a starting point, not an exhaustive inventory
- Tell you how to measure impacts — they suggest areas to consider; you must develop your own measurement plan
- Guarantee you or your work are ethical — they support ethical reasoning, but you are ultimately responsible for acting on that reflection
