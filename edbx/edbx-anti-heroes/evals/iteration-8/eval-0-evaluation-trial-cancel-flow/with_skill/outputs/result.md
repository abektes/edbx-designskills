This is a classic "high-friction" cancellation flow. While your Growth PM is correct that many SaaS companies use these patterns, the **Anti-Heroes** method helps us name exactly why they erode long-term brand trust and how to flip them into **Hero** moves that respect the user.

Here is the structured critique for your Thursday review.

### 1. Frame
*   **Design under review:** MossBowl cancellation flow (v1.0).
*   **User task:** Cancel a recurring subscription to stop being charged.
*   **User emotional state:** Decisive, possibly frustrated by cost or food waste, and likely in a hurry.
*   **Business goal:** Reduce churn and increase "save" rates through retention offers.
*   **Mode:** Evaluation.

---

### 2. Anti-Hero Hits
These are the manipulative roles the current design is playing.

| Card | What is happening | Where | Why it is anti-user |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Trap-Setter** | The cancel link is buried under an ambiguous label ("Plan health") in a sub-menu. | Settings → Plan health | It creates "asymmetric friction." It was likely one click to join, but the exit is hidden behind a scavenger hunt. |
| **Empathy Manipulator** | Using a photo of a "half-empty fridge" and "losing a streak" to trigger guilt and loss aversion. | Step 1: Full-screen takeover | It weaponizes the user's emotions against their financial interests. A "cooking streak" is a gamified metric, not a functional loss. |
| **Nickeling-and-Diming** | Hiding the "Cancel" option behind a wall of "Value Comparisons" and a discount. | Step 2: Plan comparison cards | It forces the user to do extra cognitive work (reading/scrolling) just to execute a choice they already made. |

---

### 3. Hero Counter-moves
These are the ethical alternatives that prioritize user agency and long-term loyalty.

| Paired Card | Concrete Change | Why it restores user value |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Empowerer** | Rename "Plan health" to "Subscription" and place the "Cancel" link clearly at the bottom. | Restores symmetry. If the user can find the "Upgrade" button easily, they should find "Cancel" just as easily. |
| **Life Coach** | Replace the fridge photo with a summary: "You have $X credit remaining" or "Your data is saved if you return." | Moves from *guilt* to *guidance*. It provides facts that help the user make an informed choice. |
| **Unveiler** | Show the 30% discount on Step 1 as an honest alternative: "Pause, get a discount, or cancel." | Transparency. Don't make the discount a "reward" for trying to leave; offer it as a genuine value proposition. |

---

### 4. Ethical Dialogue: Talking to the Team
Since your Growth PM mentioned "everyone does it," use this framing in the meeting to shift the conversation from *industry norms* to *brand integrity*.

**The Script:**
> "I understand that many SaaS apps use these friction points to lower churn. However, right now we are acting as a **Trap-Setter** by burying the link and an **Empathy Manipulator** by using guilt-tripping imagery. While this might save a few subs this week, it creates 'bitter churn'—where users leave feeling tricked and never come back. If we act as an **Empowerer** instead, we make the exit easy, which actually makes it feel safer for the user to resubscribe in the future."

**Follow-up Question for the PM:**
> "If we are confident in the 30% discount offer, why are we hiding the 'Cancel' button at the bottom of the page in a small grey font? If the value is there, the user will choose it without us needing to hide the exit."

---

### 5. Necessary Friction Kept
Not all friction is bad. You should keep these elements to ensure the user doesn't make a mistake:

1.  **The Confirmation Step:** Keep a "Confirm Cancellation" button. This prevents accidental clicks.
2.  **The "What Happens Next" Info:** Explicitly state: "You will still receive your boxes until [Date]" or "Your account will be deactivated immediately." This is helpful, not manipulative.
3.  **The Feedback Survey:** Asking *why* they are leaving (Cost, Quality, etc.) is fine, as long as it isn't a mandatory 10-question wall before they can click cancel.

### First Three Changes to Ship:
1.  **Rename the menu link** from "Plan health" to "Manage Subscription."
2.  **Remove the "half-empty fridge" imagery**; replace it with a neutral "We’re sorry to see you go" or a "Pause for a month" option.
3.  **Make the "Cancel anyway" button visually equal** to the "Keep plan" button. High-contrast buttons for one and "grey-on-grey" for the other is a dark pattern.
