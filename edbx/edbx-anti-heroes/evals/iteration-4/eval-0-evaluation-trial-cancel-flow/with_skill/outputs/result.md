This is a classic case of "Retention at any cost," where short-term churn metrics are being prioritized over long-term brand trust. Using the **Anti-Heroes** framework, we can move the conversation away from "it feels gross" to "here is the specific manipulative role this design is playing."

Here is your structured critique for the Thursday review.

### 1. Frame
*   **Design under review:** MossBowl Cancellation Flow (v1).
*   **User task:** Terminate a recurring payment; user is likely decisive, potentially budget-strained, or dissatisfied.
*   **Business goal:** Reduce churn and increase trial-to-paid retention.
*   **Mode:** Evaluation.

---

### 2. Anti-Hero Hits

| Card | What is happening | Where | Why it is anti-user |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Maze-Maker** | Using the vague label "Plan health" to house the cancellation link. | Settings Menu | Deceptive labeling. "Health" implies optimization or status, not termination. It forces the user to hunt for the exit. |
| **The Empathy Manipulator** | Using a "half-empty fridge" photo and "streak loss" copy to trigger guilt and loss aversion. | Step 1 (Takeover) | It weaponizes the user's emotions against their financial interests. It’s "confirm-shaming" via imagery. |
| **The Trap-Setter** | Hiding the "Cancel anyway" link at the bottom of a long scroll of comparison cards. | Step 2 (Offer) | It creates artificial physical friction. It assumes the user's "No" isn't final and forces them to work to prove it. |
| **The Shadow-Caster** | Making the "Cancel" link small and grey while the "Stay" options are high-contrast cards. | Step 2 (Offer) | Visual hierarchy is used to hide the user's intent. If a user can't see the exit, they haven't "chosen" to stay; they've been coerced. |

---

### 3. Hero Counter-Moves

| Paired Card | Concrete Change | Why it restores user value |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Unveiler** | Rename the menu item to "Subscription & Billing" or "Cancel Subscription." | Transparency. The user knows exactly where they are and where they are going. |
| **The Life Coach** | Replace the fridge photo with a neutral summary: "You have 3 days left in this cycle. Your data will be saved for 6 months." | Information over emotion. It helps the user make an informed decision without the guilt trip. |
| **The Empowerer** | Place the "Cancel" and "Keep Plan" buttons side-by-side with equal visual weight. | Autonomy. It respects the user's decision-making power and treats them like an adult. |
| **The Liberator** | If offering a discount, show it as a clear "One-click to apply and stay" vs "One-click to cancel." | Fairness. It offers the value (30% off) without making the alternative (canceling) a scavenger hunt. |

---

### 4. Ethical Dialogue: How to talk to the Growth PM

When the PM says "Every SaaS does it," they are citing industry standard dark patterns as a defense. Use these scripts to pivot the conversation:

*   **The "Brand Equity" Script:** *"While other SaaS apps use these patterns, they also suffer from high 'brand resentment.' If we act as a **Trap-Setter** now, we make it 10x harder to win this customer back in six months because the last thing they remember about MossBowl is how hard we made it to leave."*
*   **The "Symmetry" Script:** *"We make it a one-tap experience to sign up. To be an ethical product, the exit should be as easy as the entrance. Right now, we are playing the **Maze-Maker**, which creates a 'roach motel' effect that hurts our App Store ratings."*
*   **The "Data Integrity" Script:** *"If we 'save' a user by hiding the button, that's not a retention win; it's a measurement error. They still want to leave; they're just trapped. Let's be **The Unveiler** and get honest churn data so we can actually fix the product."*

---

### 5. Necessary Friction Kept

Not all friction is "Anti-Hero" behavior. You should recommend keeping these elements:

1.  **The "What happens next" info:** Keep the explanation that they lose their current pricing or that their data is deleted after X days. This is helpful friction that prevents accidental loss.
2.  **The Discount Offer:** It is perfectly fine to offer 30% off to stay. The ethical violation isn't the *offer*; it's hiding the "No" button underneath it.
3.  **The Confirmation Step:** One "Are you sure?" screen is standard to prevent accidental clicks. Just remove the "half-empty fridge" and the "Plan health" obfuscation.

**First three changes to propose on Thursday:**
1. Change "Plan health" to "Manage Subscription."
2. Remove the fridge photo and streak-loss guilt-trip.
3. Move the "Cancel anyway" link up so it's visible without scrolling.
