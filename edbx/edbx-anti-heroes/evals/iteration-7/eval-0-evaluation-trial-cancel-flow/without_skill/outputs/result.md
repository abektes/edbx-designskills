This is a classic "Dark Pattern" scenario. While your Growth PM is correct that many SaaS companies use these tactics, industry prevalence does not equal ethical design or long-term business health. In fact, regulatory bodies (like the FTC in the US and the CMA in the UK) are increasingly cracking down on these specific "roach motel" tactics.

Here is a structured ethical critique and a set of recommendations for your design review on Thursday.

---

### Part 1: The Ethical Critique (The "Dark Pattern" Audit)

You can present these four points as the core risks to MossBowl’s brand reputation and user trust.

#### 1. Obfuscation (The "Plan Health" Label)
*   **The Issue:** Placing the cancellation link under "Plan Health" is a form of **misdirection**. Users look for "Subscription," "Billing," or "Account Settings." 
*   **The Ethical Fail:** It violates the principle of **Transparency**. By hiding the exit under a euphemism, you are intentionally increasing the user's cognitive load and frustration to prevent a business loss.

#### 2. Confirmshaming & Emotional Manipulation
*   **The Issue:** The "half-empty fridge" and the "7-day cooking streak" loss.
*   **The Ethical Fail:** This is **Confirmshaming**. Using a photo of a "sad" fridge creates an artificial sense of scarcity or failure. While reminding users of a "streak" is a standard gamification tactic, using it at the moment of exit triggers **Loss Aversion** in a predatory way rather than a helpful way.

#### 3. The "Roach Motel" (Asymmetric Friction)
*   **The Issue:** It likely took one or two clicks to subscribe, but it takes multiple screens, scrolling, and hunting for a "small grey link" to leave.
*   **The Ethical Fail:** This is **Obstruction**. From an ethical standpoint, the "Exit" should be as easy to find as the "Entrance." When you make the "No" harder to say than the "Yes," you are no longer providing a service; you are holding a user hostage.

#### 4. Visual Interference & Hierarchy
*   **The Issue:** Using three large comparison cards and a tiny, low-contrast grey link at the bottom.
*   **The Ethical Fail:** This is **Visual Interference**. You are exploiting the user’s natural eye-scanning patterns to hide the path they have clearly expressed they want to take.

---

### Part 2: Rebutting the "Every SaaS Does It" Argument

When the PM says this is standard, here is your counter-argument:

1.  **The "Churn-Back" Metric:** High-friction cancellation creates "bad churn." A user who leaves easily might come back in three months when their schedule changes. A user who feels tricked or trapped will never return and will actively tell friends to avoid MossBowl.
2.  **Regulatory Risk:** The FTC’s proposed "Click to Cancel" rule mandates that cancelling a subscription must be at least as easy as signing up. MossBowl is currently creating significant legal exposure.
3.  **Brand Equity:** MossBowl (presumably) stands for nourishment and ease. A deceptive exit flow creates a "Brand-Product Mismatch." The last thing a user experiences should not be a feeling of being manipulated.

---

### Part 3: What is OK to Keep?

Ethical design doesn't mean you can't try to retain the customer. You can keep these elements if they are redesigned for honesty:

*   **The Save Offer (The 30% off):** It is ethically acceptable to offer an incentive to stay. **The catch:** This offer must be presented alongside a clear, equally weighted "Cancel" option.
*   **The Value Reminder:** Reminding the user that they have an active streak is okay, provided it’s framed as information ("Just a heads up, this will reset your streak") rather than a guilt trip.
*   **The Survey:** Asking *why* they are leaving is standard and helpful for the business, as long as it doesn't block the cancellation path.

---

### Part 4: The Proposed Redesign (The "Honest Exit")

To win the review, don't just point out flaws—offer the "Ethical Alternative."

1.  **Clear Labeling:** Rename "Plan Health" to "Subscription & Billing."
2.  **The "Single-Screen Save":**
    *   When they click cancel, show one screen. 
    *   **Left Side/Top:** "We're sorry to see you go. If you need a break, you can pause your deliveries here [Link]."
    *   **Right Side/Bottom:** "Stay for 30% off your next 3 months [Big Button]" vs. "Continue to Cancel [Clear, legible button of equal weight]."
3.  **Immediate Confirmation:** Once they click "Cancel," don't show more cards. Show a confirmation screen: "Your subscription is cancelled. You will still receive your final box on [Date]. We’d love to have you back anytime."
4.  **The "Pause" Alternative:** For a meal-kit app, the "Pause" button is the most ethical and effective retention tool. Often, people don't want to quit; they are just going on vacation.

### Summary for your Presentation:
> "Our current flow relies on **friction and frustration** to lower churn. This is a short-term win that creates long-term brand damage and legal risk. I propose we move to a **Value-Based Retention** flow: offer the discount clearly, but respect the user's autonomy by making the 'Cancel' path visible and honest. We want users to leave with a good taste in their mouth, so they feel comfortable coming back."
