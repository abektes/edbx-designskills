# Provenance — edbx-normative-design-scheme

Stage A draft (sources and licences, no downloads). One row per element the
skill attributes to a source. Locator and anchor are what could be confirmed
from pages read in this pass; otherwise Locator is `to verify` and the anchor
is marked `(guess)`. Stage B checks each anchor on the page.

Key finding: the method is the **Normative Design Scheme worksheet of the
Ethics for Designers toolkit** by Jet Gispen (c) 2017, from her TU Delft
Industrial Design Engineering graduation project. It is a free-to-download
template (the file was not downloaded or read). The UXP2 "Improving Everyday
Ethics" microsite lists it as a team method with four steps. SKILL.md credits
no creator and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` credits only "Consequentialist,
deontological and virtue ethics". Because the template itself was not read,
the central claim of SKILL.md, that Intention maps to Virtue Ethics, Effect to
Consequentialism and Design to Deontology, could not be confirmed, and the
template's own questions are not known. Everything from Step 0 to the
Normative Design Statement is absent from the pages read.

Pages were read through a fetch tool that summarises each page, so quoted
anchors are as reported by that tool.

## Licence

- **Ethics for Designers worksheet (`gispen2017normative`, `gispen2017toolkit`)**: copyright "(c) Jet Gispen 2017"; free to download, but no licence statement was found on the pages read, and the template itself was not read. Presume all rights reserved. Meaning for this repo: paraphrasing the method (assess a design goal against three ethical theories, then use the theories to generate ideas) is fine. Do not reproduce the template, its questions or its images verbatim, and do not ship a modified version of the worksheet, until the author's terms are known (contact via the site). SKILL.md currently carries lens questions that may be near-verbatim from the template; that cannot be checked until the template is read.
- **UXP2 microsite page (`carlock2021normative`)**: no licence found. Link to it, do not copy its text.
- **arXiv:2102.08909 (`chivukula2022surveying`)**: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 per the icon on the arXiv page. **NonCommercial** and **ShareAlike** would block copying its text or tables into a paid bundle and require the same licence on adaptations. Cite only. Its licence is not shown to cover the microsite or Gispen's tools.
- **Universal Methods of Ethical Design (`chivukula2025universal`)**: all rights reserved, paywalled book. Do not reproduce its text, hashtags or diagrams beyond a short citation.
- **Design Ethically toolkit (`zhou2026toolkit`)**: (c) Design Ethically, no reuse terms; cite only.
- The three ethical theories themselves are public ideas and free to describe. Nothing found forbids paraphrase; the open question is the template's own terms.

## Discrepancies

**Contradicted by the source** (pages read; they say otherwise):

1. **Missing creator.** SKILL.md names no creator and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` credits "Consequentialist, deontological and virtue ethics". Both method pages name Jet Gispen (Ethics for Designers, 2017) as the designer. Add her as the primary source.
2. **What makes the method.** SKILL.md says five moves (shared stakeholder map, Triad Conflict Matrix, Universal Law Test, Creator Impact, Normative Design Statement) are "what distinguishes the Normative Design Scheme", and that without them "the method is doing no work". The source method is a four-step worksheet (browse, write the design goal, assess with the three views, use the theories to improve the design). None of the five moves is on a page read, so they are edbx additions, not what defines the source method.

**Not found in a readable source** (could not read the full source or the source is silent):

3. **The Intention / Effect / Design mapping** (Virtue / Consequentialism / Deontology) and the template questions could not be confirmed: the pages say the views are described only in the downloadable template, which was not read. The pairing of Deontology with "Design" and Virtue with "Intention" is not the usual textbook framing of those theories (deontology is usually about duties and rules for acting), so it should be checked against the template before it is presented as Gispen's.
4. **Assess and Generate as two modes.** The source runs assessment then ideation as steps of one flow; separate modes are not on the pages read.
5. **Required counts and artifacts** (at least 5 stakeholders, 5 non-obvious harms, 6 design ideas, Creator Impact questions, Universal Law Test table, Triad matrix with 🟢🟡🔴) have no source; the source suggests 30-60 minutes with post-its.
6. **Hashtags and See Also** (Layers of Effect, Envisioning Cards, Value Voting) could not be tied to any page read, other than Layers of Effect existing in the Design Ethically toolkit. The book's contents are not visible on publisher pages, so inclusion of this method in *Universal Methods of Ethical Design* is unconfirmed.
7. **Universal Law Test and "greatest good for the greatest number"** are classical philosophy (Kant; utilitarianism) used with no citation. They are not claims about the source, but a reader cannot open a page for them.
