# The Twelve Consequence Categories

Full definitions, probing questions, and example responses for all twelve categories of the Bad Design Canvas. Use this reference when generating responses in Step 2.

---

## Cell 1: Cultural Appropriation 🌍

**Guiding question:** In what way(s) is the idea borrowed from a culture you or your team are not representative of?

**Probing questions:**
- Who created this? Who is it for?
- Are cultural elements, aesthetics, or practices borrowed without community consent or representation?
- Is the team building this from a position of cultural outsider?
- Does the product profit from cultural knowledge that belongs to a community that is not credited or compensated?
- Are sacred symbols, rituals, or practices being commercialized?

**Example responses:**
- A meditation app built by a non-Buddhist team that uses Buddhist iconography and terminology without attribution or cultural consultation — 🟡 Possible
- A fashion marketplace that sells "tribal" patterns designed by people from outside the originating cultures — 🔴 Significant
- A translation tool built by a multilingual team that credits linguistic sources — 🟢 Low/None

---

## Cell 2: Band-Aid 🩹

**Guiding question:** In what way(s) is the idea failing to recognize the root cause of the problem, instead serving as a temporary solution?

**Probing questions:**
- What is the systemic problem this product is patching over?
- Does the product address symptoms rather than causes?
- Could the product make the root problem harder to see or solve?
- Does the product create dependency on a temporary fix instead of enabling systemic change?
- Would this product exist if the root cause were actually solved?

**Example responses:**
- A mental health chatbot that treats anxiety symptoms but does nothing to address the systemic lack of access to licensed therapists — 🔴 Significant
- A food bank coordination app that makes food distribution more efficient but does not address why people are hungry — 🟡 Possible
- A platform that connects tenants with legal resources for housing disputes — 🟢 Low/None (addresses a structural gap)

---

## Cell 3: Unfair Control 🔒

**Guiding question:** In what way(s) is the idea leading to unfair control over the user/customer (i.e., privacy and data, restrictive ecosystems)?

**Probing questions:**
- What lock-in does this create?
- What data or privacy control is removed from users?
- Are users trapped in the ecosystem with no meaningful exit?
- Can users export their data and leave?
- Does the product make decisions about users without their knowledge or consent?
- What happens when the company changes the terms of service?

**Example responses:**
- A smart home system that requires proprietary hardware and makes it impossible to migrate to another platform — 🔴 Significant
- A social network that uses engagement data to algorithmically control what users see without transparent controls — 🔴 Significant
- An open-source tool with standard data export — 🟢 Low/None

---

## Cell 4: Exploitation ⚠️

**Guiding question:** In what way(s) does the new idea inappropriately expose or objectify the community it aims to serve?

**Probing questions:**
- Does the product turn users into a product?
- Are vulnerable communities being commercialized?
- Is the community being extracted from rather than served?
- Does the business model depend on users generating value they do not share in?
- Are people reduced to data points or engagement metrics?

**Example responses:**
- A health tracking app that sells anonymized health data to insurance companies — 🔴 Significant
- An educational platform that uses student performance data to train AI models without clear consent — 🔴 Significant
- A community forum owned and governed by its members — 🟢 Low/None

---

## Cell 5: Inefficiency 🔄

**Guiding question:** In what way(s) is the idea creating new inefficiencies, unnecessary complexity, confusion, or delay?

**Probing questions:**
- Does the solution create more friction than it removes?
- Are there simpler alternatives that already exist?
- Does the product require ongoing effort that disadvantages certain users?
- Is the product solving a problem that does not exist?
- Does it add layers of complexity to a process that was already working?

**Example responses:**
- A blockchain-based voting system that is slower, more complex, and less auditable than paper ballots — 🔴 Significant
- A project management tool with 47 features when the team only uses 5 — 🟡 Possible
- A streamlined API that reduces a 10-step process to 2 steps — 🟢 Low/None

---

## Cell 6: Environmental & Social Impact 🌱

**Guiding question:** In what way(s) is the idea using resources from finite sources or at risk of creating harsh conditions for workers?

**Probing questions:**
- What is the carbon/energy cost?
- What supply chain, labor, or gig economy harms does this create?
- What happens to the environment at scale?
- What finite resources does this product depend on?
- Are workers in the supply chain treated fairly?

**Example responses:**
- An AI service that requires massive GPU compute for training, with significant energy consumption and no offset — 🔴 Significant
- A physical product made from recycled materials with transparent supply chain — 🟢 Low/None
- A cloud service that runs on fossil-fuel energy with no transition plan — 🟡 Possible

---

## Cell 7: Stakeholder Abandonment 👥

**Guiding question:** In what way(s) does the idea fail to consider or address important stakeholders?

**Probing questions:**
- Who is left out of the product's benefit?
- Whose interests are sacrificed for the primary user's convenience?
- Which affected groups have no voice in the design?
- Are there secondary stakeholders who bear costs without receiving benefits?
- Does the product create externalities that affect people who are not users?

**Example responses:**
- A ride-sharing app that does not account for the impact on public transit systems and urban traffic — 🔴 Significant
- An enterprise tool designed only for managers, not for the employees who must use it — 🟡 Possible
- A product co-designed with all identified stakeholder groups — 🟢 Low/None

---

## Cell 8: Decreased Safety 🛡️

**Guiding question:** In what way(s) is the idea creating, or contributing to, unsafe conditions?

**Probing questions:**
- Physical safety: could the product cause physical harm?
- Digital safety: could it enable stalking, harassment, or data exposure?
- Psychological safety: could it cause mental health harm?
- What happens during failure modes?
- Could the product be weaponized?

**Example responses:**
- A location-sharing app that could enable stalking with no way for users to permanently disable tracking — 🔴 Significant
- A health app that provides medical advice without disclaimers or crisis resources — 🔴 Significant
- A password manager with strong encryption and breach monitoring — 🟢 Low/None

---

## Cell 9: Inappropriate 🚫

**Guiding question:** In what way(s) is the idea generally offensive or inappropriate?

**Probing questions:**
- Does the product normalize something that should not be normalized?
- Are there cultural, religious, or community contexts where this is offensive?
- Does it treat a serious topic with inappropriate levity?
- Would the product be considered inappropriate in any of its target markets?
- Does the product use humor, imagery, or language that could alienate or harm?

**Example responses:**
- A gamified app that treats real-world disasters as gameplay scenarios — 🔴 Significant
- A product with culturally insensitive default imagery — 🟡 Possible
- A thoughtfully localized product reviewed by cultural consultants — 🟢 Low/None

---

## Cell 10: Boring 😐

**Guiding question:** In what way(s) is the idea the last, plain boring?

**Probing questions:**
- Is this a derivative solution that adds nothing new?
- Does it fail to address the genuine complexity of the problem?
- Is the design so safe it is irrelevant?
- Could this idea be described as "yet another [X]"?
- Does the product lack creative ambition?

**Tone note:** This category is intentionally lighter — a creative quality check, not a moral concern. Handle with a lighter, more playful tone. A boring product is not unethical; it is just uninspired.

**Example responses:**
- A to-do list app with no differentiating features in a market saturated with them — 🟡 Possible (derivative but not harmful)
- A novel approach to an old problem that challenges assumptions — 🟢 Low/None
- A product that copies a competitor's features without understanding why they exist — 🟡 Possible

---

## Cell 11: Displacement 🏠

**Guiding question:** In what way(s) is the idea contributing to substitution or replacement in its efforts to address displacement?

**Probing questions:**
- Does this product displace workers, businesses, or communities?
- Does it replace something people valued with something inferior?
- Does it create displacement as an unintended consequence at scale?
- What existing systems, jobs, or practices does this make obsolete?
- Are the displaced parties compensated or transitioned?

**Example responses:**
- An AI writing tool that displaces freelance writers without providing alternative livelihoods — 🔴 Significant
- A digital payment system that displaces cash-based economies where vulnerable populations depend on cash — 🔴 Significant
- A tool that augments existing workflows rather than replacing them — 🟢 Low/None

---

## Cell 12: Inequity ⚖️

**Guiding question:** In what way(s) is the idea contributing to inequity (not ensuring everyone has access, no matter their unique needs or circumstances)?

**Probing questions:**
- Who cannot access this product due to cost, disability, language, or geography?
- Does the product widen existing inequalities?
- Are the benefits concentrated among already-privileged groups?
- Does the product assume a level of digital literacy, income, or connectivity that excludes significant populations?
- Are there populations for whom this product would be harmful rather than helpful?

**Example responses:**
- A telehealth platform that requires high-speed internet, excluding rural and low-income users — 🔴 Significant
- A financial app available only in English, excluding non-English speakers from financial tools — 🟡 Possible
- A free, multilingual, accessibility-compliant public service tool — 🟢 Low/None
